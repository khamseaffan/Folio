# Google and Google DeepMind agent harness evaluation

Scope: Google and Google DeepMind primary pages opened through 13 November 2026. Older posts are marked. Recommendation (what they tell others to build) is labeled separately from reported practice (what they say they ran). Quotes are short and tied to the page that was opened. Pages that were not opened are listed under Gaps and are not used as evidence.

Pages opened in full: Vertex agent-evaluation announcement (24 Jan 2025); Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform docs "Evaluate Gen AI agents" and "Manage evaluation metrics" (both last updated 2026-11-12 UTC); "A methodical approach to agent evaluation" (17 Nov 2025); ADK "Why evaluate agents", "Evaluation Criteria", and the ADK Python eval-config guide on GitHub; agents-cli evaluation guide (last updated 4 Nov 2026); "The Anatomy of Harness Engineering" (9 Sept 2026); Gemini 2.0 announcement with Project Mariner (11 Dec 2024); Gemini 2.5 Computer Use announcement (7 Oct 2025); Multimodal Knowledge Agent benchmark post (10 Nov 2026); google-research/harness2 README (arXiv:2610.14224); AutoHarness HTML (arXiv:2603.03329, Google DeepMind authors).

## What does Google say an agent harness or scaffold is, and which part of the system do they evaluate separately from the model?

### Takeaway
Google uses "harness" for the system around a model with fixed weights: prompts, tools, memory, guardrails, skills, orchestration, and the action loop. Product docs rarely use the word harness. They instead split evaluation into the trajectory (tool calls and path) and the final response (prose and goal success), and they tell builders to score both.

### Cited Findings
- Reported definition, research code, not an official product: "A harness is the system around a model that enables it to act as an agent: prompts, tools, memory, skills, orchestration, and more. Harnessing is the process of optimizing that system." Harness² "keeps model weights fixed" and edits eight components: "system prompt, guardrails, memory, tool guidance, subagents, skills, scripts, and plugins." The README says "This is not an officially supported Google product." — [Harness² README](https://github.com/google-research/harness2) (raw README opened; citation Xu et al., arXiv:2610.14224, 2026)
- Reported definition, DeepMind paper: "An agent is often defined as the combination of a specific LLM and a harness that acts as the 'glue' or 'plumbing' between the model and the task." They propose "code as harness," where the harness "can be seen as a control loop that calls the LLM and rejects unacceptable answers." Three shapes: harness-as-action-filter, harness-as-action-verifier (`is_legal_action()`), and harness-as-policy (code chooses the action, no LLM at decision time). Authors list `@deepmind.com`. — [AutoHarness](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.03329)
- Reported practice, model held fixed inside one harness: MKA runs "Every model" in "the same open-source agent harness, Stirrup, with the same tools, sandbox, and turn budget." The thing that varies is the model. Gemini 4 Carbon is reported as "#2 in quality" and "6x lower cost on average" versus the top model. — [MKA benchmark, 10 Nov 2026](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/multimodal-knowledge-agent-benchmark/)
- Reported practice, harness varied, weights fixed: one Harness² step "improves GLM-5 and Gemini 3.5 Flash over their base harnesses, with gains of up to 11.5 points on WorkBuddy-Bench, 4.5 points on JobBench, and 11.8 percentage points on LAB's partial pass rate." — [Harness² README](https://github.com/google-research/harness2)
- Recommendation, product language, not the word harness: "Unlike evaluating generative models, where the focus is primarily on the final output, agent evaluation requires a deeper understanding of the decision-making process." ADK splits this into "Evaluate Trajectory and Tool Use" and "Evaluate the Final Response." A trajectory "is just a list of steps the agent took before it returned to the user." — [Why evaluate agents, ADK](https://adk.dev/evaluate/)
- Recommendation, Vertex, older announcement: metrics "can be grouped in two categories: final response and trajectory evaluation." Final response: "does your agent achieve its goals?" Trajectory: "the agent's decision-making process." — [Vertex agent evaluation, 24 Jan 2025](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/introducing-agent-evaluation-in-vertex-ai-gen-ai-evaluation-service)
- Current product docs still use that split, and they sometimes name the model as the thing under test: trajectory metrics "help you to evaluate the model's ability to follow the expected trajectory." The same page also says the service measures "your agent's ability to complete tasks and goals." — [Evaluate Gen AI agents](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/models/evaluation-agents) (last updated 2026-11-12 UTC)
- Recommendation, engineering post about coding-agent harnesses: "Behavioral evaluations function like integration tests for improving agent harness operation." They "assert on intermediate execution steps, like specific tool calls or file modifications, instead of final string equality." End-to-end benchmarks such as Terminal-Bench and DeepSWE are described as the default for "evaluating model performance," and as insufficient for knowing why a harness change moved the score. — [Anatomy of Harness Engineering, 9 Sept 2026](https://developers.googleblog.com/the-anatomy-of-harness-engineering-how-to-evaluate-iterate-and-guard-ai-coding-agents/), Taylor Mullen and Christian Gunderman
- Reported loop, Computer Use, older: the `computer_use` tool "should be operated within a loop." Inputs are "the user request, screenshot of the environment, and a history of recent actions." The model returns a function call; "client-side code then executes the received action"; a new screenshot and URL come back as the function response. A separate "out-of-model, inference-time safety service" assesses each proposed action before execution. The blog says versions of this model powered Project Mariner, the Firebase Testing Agent, and some agentic capabilities in AI Mode in Search. It does not say they scored the client loop apart from the model. — [Gemini 2.5 Computer Use, 7 Oct 2025](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/gemini-computer-use-model/)
- Reported prototype, older: Project Mariner "understand[s] and reason[s] across information in your browser screen, including pixels and web elements" and acts "via an experimental Chrome extension." The only published score on that page is the combined agent on WebVoyager, not a model-only score. — [Gemini 2.0 announcement, 11 Dec 2024](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/google-gemini-ai-update-december-2024/)

### Inferences
- When Google wants to credit the model, it freezes the harness (MKA / Stirrup; FACTS Search was not opened, so it is not used here). When Google wants to credit the scaffold, it freezes the weights and changes prompts, tools, guardrails, or a code verifier (Harness², AutoHarness).
- ADK and Vertex are the customer-facing version of the same split, using "trajectory" and "final response" instead of "harness" and "model." The 2026 docs still slip and call trajectory matching a test of "the model's ability."
- Computer Use publishes the loop and an out-of-model safety gate as part of the system, then reports benchmark scores for "computer use tools exposed via API." That is an agent score, not a harness-only score.

### Gaps
- The Gemini 2.5 Computer Use model card and the "Gemini 2.5 Computer Use evaluation info" page named in the 7 Oct 2025 blog were not opened. The blog's numeric table is an image and was not transcribed.
- `deepmind.google/technologies/project-mariner/` was not opened. A non-Google paper attributes a second Mariner figure and a human-vote protocol to that URL. Those claims are not treated as Google's.
- Harness² `docs/BENCHMARKS.md` was not opened, so this note does not describe how JobBench, LAB, or WorkBuddy-Bench grade a deliverable.
- No opened Google page defines "scaffold" as a distinct term from "harness." ADK and agents-cli use "scaffold" for project templates, not for the agent runtime.

## Which eval suites, graders, and pass rules do they publish?

### Takeaway
The published customer graders are reference trajectory matchers (0 or 1, or precision and recall), ROUGE on the final text, LLM judges with yes/no or valid/invalid votes, adaptive rubrics that read the agent's instructions and tool declarations, and custom Python checks. Research suites add environment legality, match win rate or reward, and expert yes/no rubrics scored by an autorater. Pass is a user-set threshold on a mean, except where a metric is explicitly informational and cannot fail a case.

### Cited Findings
- Recommendation, current Vertex computation metrics for tool sequences. Each compares `predicted_trajectory` to `reference_trajectory` unless noted. `trajectory_exact_match`: identical tool calls in the same order, score 1 or 0. `trajectory_in_order_match`: all reference calls present in order, extra calls allowed, 1 or 0. `trajectory_any_order_match`: all reference calls present, order ignored, extra calls allowed, 1 or 0. `trajectory_precision`: count of predicted actions that also appear in the reference, divided by the number of predicted actions, float in [0, 1]. `trajectory_recall`: count of reference actions that also appear in the prediction, divided by the number of reference actions, float in [0, 1]. `trajectory_single_tool_use`: a named tool is present or not, 1 or 0, no order check, no count check, reference trajectory not required. Two metrics are added without being requested: `latency` in seconds, and `failure` equal to 1 on error and 0 when a valid response returns. Aggregate tables report mean and standard deviation. — [Evaluate Gen AI agents](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/models/evaluation-agents)
- Recommendation, same page, model-based and code graders. A documented pointwise judge, `response_follows_trajectory`, rates 1 for "Follows trajectory" and 0 for "Does not follow trajectory," using prompt and predicted trajectory. A documented custom function, `essential_tools_present`, returns the fraction of a required tool list that appears in the predicted trajectory. Final-response text metrics such as `rouge_l_sum` and `bleu` can be mixed into the same `EvalTask`. Public demo sets named on the page: `on-device`, `customer-support`, `content-creation`, under `gs://cloud-ai-demo-datasets/agent-eval-datasets/`. The feature is marked Preview / Pre-GA. — [Evaluate Gen AI agents](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/models/evaluation-agents)
- Recommendation, current rubric metrics in the metric registry. Single-turn: `FINAL_RESPONSE_QUALITY` (adaptive rubric "auto-generates rubric criteria based on the agent's configuration (system instructions and tool declarations) and the user's prompt"); `HALLUCINATION` (static rubric, "segmenting the response into atomic claims and verifying each claim is grounded in the tool usage from intermediate events"); `TOOL_USE_QUALITY` (adaptive rubric, "selection of appropriate tools, correct parameter usage, and adherence to the specified sequence of operations"); `SAFETY` (static rubric, returns 1 for safe and 0 for unsafe; policies listed include PII, demographic data, hate speech, dangerous content, harassment, sexually explicit content). Multi-turn: `MULTI_TURN_TASK_SUCCESS` ("reference-free," "whether the goal was achieved, not how"); `MULTI_TURN_TOOL_USE_QUALITY` ("right tools with correct arguments at the right time"); `MULTI_TURN_TRAJECTORY_QUALITY` ("how the agent achieved the goal," "logical and efficient"). Custom metrics are either an `LLMMetric` ("Judge LLM") or a `CodeExecutionMetric` (Python). The registry is described as applying "to both offline assessments and continuous online monitors." — [Manage evaluation metrics](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/optimize/evaluation/manage-metrics) (last updated 2026-11-12 UTC)
- Recommendation, ADK criteria table (Python). Deterministic, reference-based, no judge: `tool_trajectory_avg_score` (tool-call trajectory; default `EXACT`); `response_match_score` (ROUGE-1 unigram overlap with the reference response). Judge, reference-based: `final_response_match_v2` (semantic match; judge says "valid" or "invalid"). Judge, rubric, reference-free: `rubric_based_final_response_quality_v1`, `rubric_based_tool_use_quality_v1`, `rubric_based_multi_turn_trajectory_quality_v1`. Judge, no user rubric list: `hallucinations_v1`, `safety_v1`, `per_turn_user_simulator_quality_v1`, `multi_turn_task_success_v1`, `multi_turn_trajectory_quality_v1`, `multi_turn_tool_use_quality_v1`. `response_evaluation_score` is a Vertex coherence score on a 1 to 5 scale. Per invocation, trajectory match is 1.0 or 0.0; the case score is the average across invocations. Pass: the mean is at least the configured threshold. Default config if none is provided: `tool_trajectory_avg_score` 1.0 and `response_match_score` 0.8. Example thresholds in the docs, not universal pass rules: final-response match 0.8, tool-use rubric 1.0, multi-turn trajectory rubric 0.7. — [ADK criteria](https://adk.dev/evaluate/criteria/); [ADK why evaluate](https://adk.dev/evaluate/); [ADK eval config](https://github.com/google/adk-python/blob/main/docs/guides/evaluation/eval_config/index.md)
- Recommendation, ADK argument matchers. `tool_parameter_match` gives partial credit per argument. `match_type` defaults to `IN_ORDER` for this metric (stricter pairing than trajectory `IN_ORDER`). `args_matcher` examples: `casefold`, `numeric` with a tolerance (example 0.5), and `ignore`. Without a matcher, arguments must be equal. `EXACT` on `tool_trajectory_avg_score` fails any deviation in tool name, arguments, or order. — [ADK eval config](https://github.com/google/adk-python/blob/main/docs/guides/evaluation/eval_config/index.md)
- Recommendation, ADK judge aggregation. For `final_response_match_v2` and the rubric criteria, the judge is sampled `num_samples` times and a majority vote sets the invocation or rubric score. Default `judge_model` is `gemini-2.5-flash`. Default `num_samples` is 5. The config guide says a single judge call is unreliable and that five samples "aggregate into a stable score." Rubrics are yes/no (1.0 or 0.0). The invocation score is the average of rubric scores. For multi-turn custom trajectory rubrics, only the last turn carries the score; earlier turns are `NOT_EVALUATED`. — [ADK criteria](https://adk.dev/evaluate/criteria/); [ADK eval config](https://github.com/google/adk-python/blob/main/docs/guides/evaluation/eval_config/index.md)
- Recommendation, ADK efficiency metrics never decide a pass. `tool_call_count_v1`, `inference_call_count_v1`, `token_usage_v1`, and `invocation_duration_v1` are always reported, status `INFORMATIONAL`, and a threshold set on them is rejected. — [ADK criteria](https://adk.dev/evaluate/criteria/)
- Recommendation, agents-cli starter metrics, current. Tool-calling single-turn: `tool_use_quality`, `final_response_quality`. RAG: `hallucination`, `safety`, and `grounding` when the case has `context`. Conversational single-turn: `general_quality`, `instruction_following`, `text_quality`. Multi-turn goal-oriented: `multi_turn_task_success`, `multi_turn_trajectory_quality`, `multi_turn_tool_use_quality`. `final_response_match` needs a `reference`. `final_response_reference_free` needs `rubric_groups`. Only the three `multi_turn_*` metrics accept multi-turn traces. Grading "uses the GenAI Eval SDK in Gemini platform." CI pass rule: `thresholds` are minimum mean scores "in that metric's own scale." Example verdict line: `RESULT: FAIL (response_quality_rubric 3.20 < 4.00)`. The process exits 1 if a metric misses its threshold or if any case errored. An LLM judge may set `judge_model_sampling_count` from 1 to 32. Example judge model in the docs: `gemini-3.8-flash`. — [agents-cli evaluation guide](https://google.github.io/agents-cli/guide/evaluation/) (last updated 4 Nov 2026)
- Recommendation, three-pillar framing, older. Pillar 1, outcome: interaction correctness, task completion rate, conversation groundedness, coherence, relevance. Pillar 2, trajectory: tool selection accuracy, reasoning logic, efficiency. Pillar 3, trust: robustness, resistance to prompt injection, fairness. Methods: human evaluation for ground truth, LLM-as-a-judge after it is aligned to humans, and "simple Python functions" for objective checks. An example quality gate converts human feedback into binary pass/fail dimensions and then a letter grade ("A" all binary tests pass, "B" two thirds, "C" one third). That letter scale is an illustration in the post, not a product default. — [A methodical approach to agent evaluation, 17 Nov 2025](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/developers-practitioners/a-methodical-approach-to-agent-evaluation), Hugo Selbie
- Reported practice, MKA, current. 200 private tasks, 10 public tasks on Hugging Face. Each task is "a multi-step project that takes a seasoned professional three to eight hours," with "an expert-written rubric of roughly 70 yes/no checks, weighted 75% on factual accuracy and 25% on visual design." "An agentic autorater built on a non-Google model" opens files, renders pages, inspects spreadsheet formulas, and checks the rubric. "When domain experts graded model outputs themselves, they agreed with the autorater 96% of the time." Models used "between 30 and 130 turns on average." Artificial Analysis keeps the 200-task set private. — [MKA, 10 Nov 2026](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/multimodal-knowledge-agent-benchmark/)
- Reported practice, Mariner, older. "When evaluated against the WebVoyager benchmark, which tests agent performance on end-to-end real world web tasks, Project Mariner achieved a state-of-the-art result of 83.5% working as a single agent setup." The post does not define the grader, the number of raters, the pass rule, or a second score. — [Gemini 2.0, 11 Dec 2024](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/google-gemini-ai-update-december-2024/)
- Reported practice, Computer Use, older. The blog says results mix "self-reported numbers, evaluations run by Browserbase and evaluations we ran ourselves," and that "scores shown are for computer use tools exposed via API." It says the model "offers leading quality for browser control at the lowest latency, as measured by performance on the Browserbase harness for Online-Mind2Web." No percentage is in the fetched text. — [Gemini 2.5 Computer Use, 7 Oct 2025](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/gemini-computer-use-model/)
- Reported practice, AutoHarness, DeepMind. Training target for the verifier harness is legal-action rate, stopped at 1.0 or timeout. "100% legal action success rate" on 145 TextArena games (9 free-form-text games excluded) on held-out rollouts of length 1000 across 10 seeds. Average 14.5 tree-search iterations; 19 of 32 games under 10 iterations. Play evaluation, not legality: 16 one-player games, 20 matches, reward; 16 two-player games, 40 matches, win/draw/loss, same prompt for all agents. Flash+harness vs Gemini-2.5-Pro: two-player overall win rate 56.3% vs 38.2% (9 of 16 games won); vs vanilla Flash, 64.8% and 12 of 16. One-player mean reward 0.745 vs 0.707 (Pro) and 0.673 (Flash), higher in 8 of 16 and tied in 5 of 16. Harness-as-policy mean reward 0.870 vs GPT-5.2-High 0.844, Gemini-2.5-Pro 0.707, GPT-5.2 0.635; training heuristic 0.939 after 89.4 iterations on average, cap 256. The paper says 78% of Gemini-2.5-Flash losses in the Kaggle GameArena chess competition were illegal moves. That 78% is attributed to the competition, not measured in this paper's tables. — [AutoHarness](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.03329)

### Inferences
- Customer pass rules are thresholds on means. Google does not publish one universal cutoff. The only defaults found are ADK's 1.0 trajectory and 0.8 ROUGE when the caller sets nothing.
- Outcome graders and trajectory graders are separate products. `MULTI_TURN_TASK_SUCCESS` is explicitly about whether the goal was met, not how. Trajectory and tool-use metrics are the how.
- Adaptive rubrics grade the agent against its own system instructions and tool declarations. That is a check on whether behavior matches the configured harness, not a check against a human reference trace.
- Research pass rules that were actually used in a Google run, on opened pages, are: WebVoyager success rate (protocol not specified on the Google post), MKA rubric agreement, AutoHarness legal-move rate of 1.0 during harness search and then match reward or win rate, and Harness² point gains on three external benches (grader internals not opened).

### Gaps
- Computer Use numeric scores (Online-Mind2Web, WebVoyager, AndroidWorld, pass@1) were not taken from an opened Google page. Secondary writeups and a third-party leaderboard CSV were seen in search and not used.
- The Jan 2025 Vertex post's six trajectory metrics match the current docs. Whether argument equality is required inside "tool call" identity is specified in the docs' examples (`tool_name` plus `tool_input`) but the metric prose says "tool calls" and "actions" without a formal equality rule for nested arguments. ADK is more explicit.
- Per-rubric weights inside Vertex adaptive metrics, the judge model those managed metrics call, and the sampling count for managed Agent Platform rubrics were not on the opened metric-registry page.
- Harness² benchmark grading rules were not opened.
- The advanced ADK "LLM-as-a-judge" codelab was returned by search and not read. It is not cited.

## Do they run those checks in CI, offline, or only for model releases? What do they say about variance and a judge model deciding a pass?

### Takeaway
Customer docs tell builders to run deterministic trajectory checks in CI and to treat a judge as a sampled vote, not one call. One 2026 engineering post about Google's own coding-agent work says the opposite of a hard PR gate: do not block a pull request on a single noisy run, batch the evals, and watch the aggregate. Research announcements (Mariner, Computer Use, MKA) read as release-time or benchmark-time reports. None of the opened pages say Google's Gemini training or release process gates on ADK or Vertex agent metrics.

### Cited Findings
- Recommendation, ADK CI. "Due to the probabilistic nature of models, deterministic 'pass/fail' assertions are often unsuitable." Then, for CI specifically: "Enable tests in CI/CD pipelines or regression testing: Use `tool_trajectory_avg_score` and `response_match_score`. These criteria are fast, predictable, and suitable for frequent automated checks." `pytest` via `AgentEvaluator.evaluate` "allows you to integrate agent evaluations into your CI/CD pipelines." `adk eval` "helps with automation, i.e. you can add this command as a part of your regular build generation and verification process." `adk conformance test` "fails if things don't match" and "You can set it up to run automatically whenever someone opens a pull request, blocking any code from merging if it changes the agent's expected behavior." Efficiency metrics "add no new failure mode to a CI/CD run." — [Why evaluate agents, ADK](https://adk.dev/evaluate/)
- Recommendation, ADK variance of the agent under test. "`multi_run_config` runs each eval case more than once. An agent can answer the same case differently from one run to the next, so a single run can pass or fail by chance." Default combination is the mean of per-invocation scores across runs, so "A case can therefore pass with a failing run among its runs." `min_over_runs` passes only if every run met the threshold. `pass_at_k` is "the chance that at least one of k runs passes." `pass_hat_k` is "the chance that all k runs pass." "A `pass_hat_k` well below `pass_at_k` marks a case that the agent can pass but not consistently, which is flaky rather than wrong." If neither CLI nor config sets `num_runs`, `adk eval` runs each case once. `AgentEvaluator` (the pytest path) defaults `num_runs` to 2 and "does not read `multi_run_config`." `adk web` ignores the cross-run keys. Crashed runs: `max_crashed_runs` defaults to 0, and a crash fails the case under that default. — [ADK eval config](https://github.com/google/adk-python/blob/main/docs/guides/evaluation/eval_config/index.md)
- Recommendation, judge variance. ADK: majority vote over `num_samples`, default 5, because "a single judge call is unreliable." agents-cli: `judge_model_sampling_count` between 1 and 32. The agents-cli page does not say how the samples are reduced to one score. — [ADK eval config](https://github.com/google/adk-python/blob/main/docs/guides/evaluation/eval_config/index.md); [agents-cli evaluation guide](https://google.github.io/agents-cli/guide/evaluation/)
- Recommendation, agents-cli CI. "`eval run` and `eval grade` ... exit 1 when a metric misses its threshold, so CI can gate on the exit code." `eval submit` to the Eval Service is marked experimental and described as "For large datasets or CI." The guide says to expect "5 to 10 or more cycles before your agent consistently passes." — [agents-cli evaluation guide](https://google.github.io/agents-cli/guide/evaluation/)
- Recommendation, CI as a quality gate, older. "Your evaluation suite should act as a quality gate that runs automatically with every proposed change to the agent." The pipeline "computes key metrics, and compares the scores against predefined thresholds. If performance scores fall below the threshold, the build fails." Before deployment, "align the LLM judge to the human evaluation by comparing the judge's output against the original manual human output." The post also says a golden labeled set is not required to start. — [A methodical approach, 17 Nov 2025](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/developers-practitioners/a-methodical-approach-to-agent-evaluation)
- Recommendation that conflicts with a hard single-run PR gate, and is written as their own practice. "Rather than blocking PRs on single eval runs that can be noisy due to nondeterminism of AI models, automate batch evaluations to pull a larger volume of data. Tracking aggregate pass rates over time ensures the model's behavior is trending correctly." "Evals belong to the second phase of development," after the agent can dogfood its own codebase. The local example is `pytest evals/behavioral/`, "in under 5 seconds." They also describe a loop "where an LLM tweaks its own system prompt, iterating until a failing test finally passes, all while the rest of your test suite acts similar to how a CI/CD-style guardrail operates." — [Anatomy of Harness Engineering, 9 Sept 2026](https://developers.googleblog.com/the-anatomy-of-harness-engineering-how-to-evaluate-iterate-and-guard-ai-coding-agents/)
- Recommendation, offline plus online. The metric registry is for "offline assessments and continuous online monitors." Production monitoring in the Nov 2025 post is separate from CI: tool-call error rates, API latency, tokens per interaction, thumbs up/down, conversation length, task completion, and drift. — [Manage evaluation metrics](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/optimize/evaluation/manage-metrics); [A methodical approach, 17 Nov 2025](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/developers-practitioners/a-methodical-approach-to-agent-evaluation)
- Reported practice, release or benchmark time, not CI. Mariner's 83.5% is in a model-family announcement. Computer Use scores are in a model launch post and point at a model card. MKA is a benchmark launch with a private set held by Artificial Analysis "so the tasks can't leak into training data or be found by the model via search." None of these posts say the suite runs in CI. — [Gemini 2.0, 11 Dec 2024](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/google-gemini-ai-update-december-2024/); [Computer Use, 7 Oct 2025](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/gemini-computer-use-model/); [MKA, 10 Nov 2026](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/multimodal-knowledge-agent-benchmark/)
- Reported practice, judge family bias, MKA. The autorater is "built on a non-Google model." Agreement with domain experts is 96% on the outputs those experts graded. The post does not say how many judge samples, whether a disagreement fails the task, or the variance of the autorater. — [MKA, 10 Nov 2026](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/multimodal-knowledge-agent-benchmark/)
- Reported practice, Vertex result tables include "standard deviation" across instances. That is spread across the dataset, not a statement about rerunning one case. — [Evaluate Gen AI agents](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/models/evaluation-agents)
- Reported practice, AutoHarness variance control is repeated matches (20 or 40) and 10 random seeds on the legality test, not an LLM judge. The environment is the grader for legality. — [AutoHarness](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.03329)

### Inferences
- Google's customer stack and Google's coding-agent engineering post do not give the same CI policy. ADK, agents-cli, and the Nov 2025 Cloud post say a threshold failure should fail the build. The Sept 2026 Developers Blog says a single eval run is too noisy to block a PR, and that the signal is the aggregate trend.
- A judge does not, in the ADK design, decide a pass from one sample. The pass is the mean of invocation scores, and each judged invocation is a majority vote. agents-cli can set the sample count to 1, so a one-call judge is allowed there.
- Nothing opened shows these agent graders inside Gemini's model-release pipeline. The model posts report external or partner benchmarks at launch.

### Gaps
- No opened page states the sampling temperature of the judge, the vote tie-break, or a measured judge-human agreement number for ADK or Vertex managed metrics. MKA's 96% is the only agreement figure opened.
- No opened page says whether Google's internal Gemini or Mariner development runs ADK evals, Vertex `EvalTask`, or agents-cli in CI.
- "Continuous online monitors" are named in the metric-registry docs. The monitor schedule, the alert threshold, and what happens on a breach were not on that page.

## What concrete mechanisms do they describe for evaluating the harness rather than the prose?

### Takeaway
The concrete mechanism is to score the tool trace, the code that accepts or rejects an action, or a behavior assertion, and to keep the final sentence out of the pass rule. Google also describes the opposite experiment, one shared harness, when the prose or the deliverable is what they want to compare across models.

### Cited Findings
- Recommendation, assert on the call, not the sentence. The harness-engineering example checks `types.BuiltinTools.SEARCH_WEB` is in `response.tool_calls` and comments "Assert behavior, not output prose." Other checks they name: an underspecified prompt should produce a clarifying question; a build-file edit should run the local validator before completion; generated docs should include canonical repository links. "For simple tasks with one optimal solution, build a strict single-turn assertion." "For more complex tasks ... avoid enforcing a rigid tool sequence" and "use fuzzier, outcome-based checks, such as an LLM-as-a-judge." — [Anatomy of Harness Engineering, 9 Sept 2026](https://developers.googleblog.com/the-anatomy-of-harness-engineering-how-to-evaluate-iterate-and-guard-ai-coding-agents/)
- Recommendation, recorded trace as ground truth. An ADK eval turn stores "Expected Intermediate Tool Use Trajectory," "Expected Intermediate Agent Responses," and "Final Response." The web UI saves a live session into the eval set. Trace view shows Event, Request, Response, and Graph for tool calls. User simulation is incompatible with `tool_trajectory_avg_score`, `response_match_score`, and `final_response_match_v2`, because those need expected tool use or expected responses. Rubric and multi-turn metrics can run with a simulated user. — [Why evaluate agents, ADK](https://adk.dev/evaluate/); [ADK criteria](https://adk.dev/evaluate/criteria/)
- Recommendation, match types as harness tolerances. `EXACT` fails extra calls. `IN_ORDER` allows extra calls if the expected ones stay in order. `ANY_ORDER` allows extra calls and any order. The config guide: "Each step away from `EXACT` buys tolerance for a variation you consider harmless and gives up the ability to catch that variation when it is not." `include_intermediate_responses_in_final` defaults to false, so narration before a tool call is not scored unless turned on. `hallucinations_v1` can be pointed at intermediate natural-language responses with `evaluate_intermediate_nl_responses` (default false). — [ADK eval config](https://github.com/google/adk-python/blob/main/docs/guides/evaluation/eval_config/index.md)
- Recommendation, custom code on the trace. ADK `custom_metrics` example: fail if any `set_temperature` call is outside 18 to 30 Celsius. Vertex `essential_tools_present` scores tool presence as a fraction. agents-cli code metrics implement `evaluate(instance)` and can run locally or `execution: remote` in the platform sandbox. The scaffolded judge metric is `custom_response_quality` in `tests/eval/response_quality.py`, which is prose grading, not a harness check. — [ADK eval config](https://github.com/google/adk-python/blob/main/docs/guides/evaluation/eval_config/index.md); [Evaluate Gen AI agents](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/models/evaluation-agents); [agents-cli evaluation guide](https://google.github.io/agents-cli/guide/evaluation/)
- Recommendation, separate "silent failure" from a correct sentence. "An agent can produce a correct output through an inefficient or incorrect process." Example: inventory number is right, but the agent "reference[d] last year's report." Trajectory review is the diagnostic. — [A methodical approach, 17 Nov 2025](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/developers-practitioners/a-methodical-approach-to-agent-evaluation)
- Reported practice, environment as the harness grader. AutoHarness trains on "average legal move accuracy." A rollout stops on an illegal move or a code failure. The critic feeds at most 5 failed steps back to the refiner. Test rollouts measure "the fraction of legal actions," and they report 100% on all 145 games. Play strength is a second metric, after the harness already passes the legality gate. Same prompt across Flash, Pro, and Flash+harness, so the reported lift is the verifier, not a new prompt. — [AutoHarness](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.03329)
- Reported practice, edit the harness, rescore the task. Harness² task-level step is "Propose–Probe–Compose": "propose edits, probe their effects on the same task, and compose a refined harness from behavioral contrasts between executions, without ground-truth feedback." The command sequence is `harness2` then `harness2-judge` then `harness2-report`, with the same benchmark, executor, subset, `--k`, `--mode`, and seed. The README says executor network is unrestricted and "Public benchmark rubrics may be accessible online," and tells the user to restrict the network "to prevent access to benchmark answers and grading criteria" before evaluation. — [Harness² README](https://github.com/google-research/harness2)
- Reported practice, harness frozen so the model is what moves. MKA: one harness, same tools, sandbox, and turn budget. The autorater grades deliverables (files, pages, spreadsheet formulas) against the expert rubric, not the trace. The post does say models "work in very different ways" and cites turn counts from 30 to 130 as an efficiency difference under that fixed harness. — [MKA, 10 Nov 2026](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/multimodal-knowledge-agent-benchmark/)
- Reported practice, out-of-model gate described, not scored. Computer Use: per-step safety service before execution, and system instructions that "refuse or ask for user confirmation" on high-stakes actions. Mariner, older: "can only type, scroll or click in the active tab" and "asks users for final confirmation before taking certain sensitive actions, like purchasing something." Neither post reports a pass rate for those gates. — [Computer Use, 7 Oct 2025](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/gemini-computer-use-model/); [Gemini 2.0, 11 Dec 2024](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/google-gemini-ai-update-december-2024/)
- Reported memory bound, not an eval. Project Astra "now has up to 10 minutes of in-session memory." The Dec 2024 post does not say they evaluated that memory module apart from the model. — [Gemini 2.0, 11 Dec 2024](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/google-gemini-ai-update-december-2024/)

### Inferences
- The mechanisms that actually ignore prose are: exact or ordered tool-trace match, single-tool presence, argument matchers, custom Python over the trace, pytest asserts on tool names, and an environment legality checker.
- LLM judges are used on traces as well as on prose (`TOOL_USE_QUALITY`, `rubric_based_tool_use_quality_v1`, `response_follows_trajectory`). Those still depend on a model reading the trace.
- Holding the harness constant (MKA, and the Computer Use "Browserbase harness" comparison) is how they stop the scaffold from confounding a model score. It is not a harness eval.

### Gaps
- No opened page shows a memory-store eval, a gate-pass rate, or a prompt-ablation table from a Gemini product launch. Harness² claims prompt and memory are editable components. The component-level ablation was not in the README.
- Computer Use "evaluation info" was not opened, so it is unknown whether that page separates the client loop from the model.
- Intermediate agent responses are stored in ADK eval cases. The opened criteria pages do not name a deterministic grader that diffs those intermediate prose strings the way ROUGE diffs the final response.

## What do they explicitly advise others to do, versus what they report from their own runs?

### Takeaway
Advice to builders is: define success first, score trajectory and final response separately, start with cheap deterministic checks, add a judge only where meaning matters, sample the judge, set a threshold, and run the suite on change. What they report from their own runs is different in each program: Mariner and Computer Use publish an end-to-end agent score, MKA freezes one harness and grades deliverables with a non-Google autorater, AutoHarness and Harness² change the harness and keep the weights fixed, and the coding-agent post says they do not block PRs on one noisy eval.

### Cited Findings
- Advice, not a report of an internal score. Vertex Jan 2025 and the current docs tell the reader to supply prompt, reference trajectory, predicted trajectory, and response, then choose exact, in-order, any-order, precision, recall, or single-tool metrics, plus custom judges. They do not publish Google's own pass rates on those metrics. — [Vertex announcement, 24 Jan 2025](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/introducing-agent-evaluation-in-vertex-ai-gen-ai-evaluation-service); [Evaluate Gen AI agents](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/models/evaluation-agents)
- Advice. ADK: start from the deterministic pair, threshold 1.0 and 0.8 if you set nothing, put those two in CI, use a judge when you need meaning, use rubrics when you have no reference, do not let cost metrics fail the build, capture sessions in the UI, and use conformance tests to block merges that change expected behavior. The pages do not say the Gemini team runs this. — [Why evaluate agents](https://adk.dev/evaluate/); [ADK criteria](https://adk.dev/evaluate/criteria/); [ADK eval config](https://github.com/google/adk-python/blob/main/docs/guides/evaluation/eval_config/index.md)
- Advice. agents-cli: one or two core cases first, `eval run`, fix instructions or tools, `eval compare`, then edge cases, 5 to 10 cycles, gate CI on mean thresholds. `eval optimize` (GEPA prompt optimization) is experimental, ADK Python only, and "after manual fixes stop helping." — [agents-cli evaluation guide](https://google.github.io/agents-cli/guide/evaluation/)
- Advice. Nov 2025 Cloud post: write a measurable success statement, cover outcome, trajectory, and adversarial pillars, ground the judge in human labels, generate data with a user-role LLM and anonymized production logs, fail CI below a threshold, and feed production failures back into the set. The letter-grade example is illustrative. — [A methodical approach, 17 Nov 2025](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/developers-practitioners/a-methodical-approach-to-agent-evaluation)
- Advice presented as what helped their coding-agent work. Dogfood before building an eval harness. Behavioral asserts on tool calls. Do not block PRs on one run. Batch and track pass rate. Keep end-to-end benchmarks as a complement, not a replacement. The code sample imports `google.antigravity`. The post does not publish a pass rate, a suite size, or a judge-agreement number from those runs. — [Anatomy of Harness Engineering, 9 Sept 2026](https://developers.googleblog.com/the-anatomy-of-harness-engineering-how-to-evaluate-iterate-and-guard-ai-coding-agents/)
- Reported, not a how-to. Mariner 83.5% WebVoyager as a single agent, 11 Dec 2024. Human confirmation before purchase and active-tab-only actions are safety constraints, not eval metrics. — [Gemini 2.0, 11 Dec 2024](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/google-gemini-ai-update-december-2024/)
- Reported, not a how-to. Computer Use launch, 7 Oct 2025: loop, out-of-model per-step safety service, Browserbase harness used for a latency and quality comparison, scores deferred to a model card and a Browserbase post. Developers are told to "thoroughly test their systems before launch." That sentence is advice. The benchmark claim is a report, with the numbers not in the fetched text. — [Computer Use, 7 Oct 2025](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/gemini-computer-use-model/)
- Reported, with a method others can inspect. MKA, 10 Nov 2026: 200 private tasks, about 70 yes/no checks, 75/25 factual/design weight, same Stirrup harness, non-Google autorater, 96% agreement with domain experts, 30 to 130 turns, Gemini 4 Carbon second in quality and 6x lower cost than the top model, 10 public tasks. The post invites others to run models on Artificial Analysis. It does not say Google uses Stirrup or this autorater in CI. — [MKA, 10 Nov 2026](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/multimodal-knowledge-agent-benchmark/)
- Reported, research, weights fixed. Harness² point gains cited above. Disclaimer: not an officially supported Google product. The README advises restricting network access before judging so the agent cannot fetch the rubric. That is an instruction to users of the repo, and also a description of a contamination control they say matters. — [Harness² README](https://github.com/google-research/harness2)
- Reported, research. AutoHarness numbers in the previous section. The comparison that isolates the harness is Flash versus Flash plus the learned verifier, same prompt, environment legality as the gate, then match outcomes as the quality metric. — [AutoHarness](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.03329)

### Inferences
- The advice is more specific than the launch reports. Builders are told how to pass or fail a case. Mariner and Computer Use tell the reader a headline success rate and do not tell the reader the grader.
- The only opened first-party account of Google engineers evaluating their own coding harness, as opposed to shipping an eval product, is the 9 Sept 2026 post. It prefers fast deterministic behavior checks and aggregate trends, and it does not claim they fail merges on one LLM-judge score.
- Where they report a harness effect (AutoHarness, Harness²), the model weights stay put and the score moves. Where they report a model effect (MKA, and the wording of the Computer Use launch), the harness stays put.

### Gaps
- No opened page reports Google's internal CI pass rate, flake rate, or judge model for Gemini agents, ADK samples, or Mariner.
- Project Astra, Jules, and the Gemini 2.0 game agents are described in the Dec 2024 post. No eval suite, grader, or score for those prototypes was on that page. Jules is pointed at a developer blog that was not opened.
- FACTS Grounding and the FACTS Benchmark Suite appeared in search. Those pages were not opened, so their judge panel and "same search tool for every model" claim are not used here.
- Double-blind Gemini evaluation (Confidential Space, Singapore AISI) appeared in search and was not opened. It is about protecting eval prompts from the model provider, not about harness grading.
