# Report sections besides the prose and the source list

Comparison as of 11 November 2026. A "documented section" below is one named by the publisher, a university template, or a person describing a file they exported. A heading that appears only because a prompt asked for it is treated as question-shaped, not as a fixed part of the product.

## Which sections appear in almost every serious report?

### Takeaway
University guides for a full research report converge on a title, an introduction that states the aim, a methods account, a findings or results section, interpretation in a discussion and or a conclusion, and a references list. A short front summary is standard in scientific reports and in research briefings, and is treated as a preliminary in longer general reports.

### Cited Findings
- The University of the Sunshine Coast describes a scientific report as IMRaD: title, abstract, introduction, methods, results, and discussion, with the conclusion often the last paragraph of the discussion rather than its own chapter. The abstract is "a bit of background, your basic research question, summary of main methods, and basic conclusion." Results report what was found and are kept separate from what the findings mean. — [University of the Sunshine Coast, Scientific reports](https://libguides.usc.edu.au/scireport/imrad)
- Wits University's clinical-medicine guide lists six sections: abstract, introduction and literature review, materials and methods, results, discussion, and conclusion. The conclusion summarises main findings and also states implications, limitations, and future directions. The abstract covers purpose, materials and methods, results, a brief discussion, and conclusions. — [Wits, Guidelines on Writing a Well-structured Research Report](https://www.wits.ac.za/media/wits-university/faculties-and-schools/health-sciences/clinical-medicine/documents/Guidelines%20on%20Writing%20a%20Well%20Structured%20Research%20Report.pdf)
- A Hull skills guide, hosted by Grimsby, says a simple report is likely to include at least: introduction, including aims and objectives; methodology; findings or results; discussion; conclusions and recommendations; references. A more complex report adds preliminaries (title page, terms of reference, contents, list of tables and diagrams, acknowledgements, summary) and supplementaries (references or bibliography, appendices, glossary). — [Report Writing, A Quick Guide (Hull, via Grimsby)](https://grimsby.ac.uk/documents/highereducation/skills/Report-Writing-A-Brief-Guide-Hull.pdf)
- Edinburgh's research-briefing guide (October 2016) suggests title, summary of about 100 words, key points or findings or recommendations, and introduction, plus some of: boxes, cases, tables, graphics, photographs, and quotations. Length is generally 2 to 8 sides. — [University of Edinburgh, Write a research briefing](https://edwebcontent.ed.ac.uk/sites/default/files/atoms/files/how_to_write_a_research_briefing_oct2016.pdf)
- Simon Fraser University's research-brief template (up to 1,500 words plus references and project details) names: title, authors, summary of the problem, description of the research, research findings, implications, further research, and key messages. Methods inside the description "should only take up 2-3 sentences." — [SFU STAR Institute, Research Brief template](http://www.sfu.ca/content/dam/sfu/starinstitute/Documents/STAR%20Institute%20Real-World%20Output%20Templates%20and%20Guidlines%20Tool-kit%20(1).pdf)

### Inferences
- The overlap across the full-report guides is the title, a statement of the question or aim, how the work was done, what was found, what that means, and a source list. The front summary is nearly as common, but the shortest Hull outline puts the summary with the longer report's preliminaries, so it is not in every short template.
- Briefings keep the title, a summary, findings, and a closing judgment (implications or key messages). They do not keep a full methods chapter.

### Gaps
- These are teaching templates, not a survey of published journal articles or of government briefings in 2025-2026. They do not show how often a finished professional report drops the methods heading.

## Which sections are optional and depend on the question?

### Takeaway
Recommendations, a standalone literature review, quotations, appendices, a glossary, acknowledgements, and a separate limitations or future-research heading are template-dependent. In the four product reports, the body headings follow the question. None of the official help pages publishes one outline that every downloaded report must use.

### Cited Findings
- Hull says recommendations are added "if you have been asked to," and lists title page, terms of reference, contents, list of tables, acknowledgements, summary, appendices, and glossary as parts of the more complex report, not of the simple one. — [Hull report guide](https://grimsby.ac.uk/documents/highereducation/skills/Report-Writing-A-Brief-Guide-Hull.pdf)
- Wits folds the literature review into the introduction, and folds limitations and future directions into the conclusion, so those are not required as separate headings in that guide. — [Wits research-report guide](https://www.wits.ac.za/media/wits-university/faculties-and-schools/health-sciences/clinical-medicine/documents/Guidelines%20on%20Writing%20a%20Well%20Structured%20Research%20Report.pdf)
- The Sunshine Coast guide says the conclusion is often the last paragraph of the discussion. — [University of the Sunshine Coast](https://libguides.usc.edu.au/scireport/imrad)
- SFU asks for a few brief quotes only "if this is qualitative research," and for implications and further-research questions as their own short blocks in a briefing. — [SFU research-brief template](http://www.sfu.ca/content/dam/sfu/starinstitute/Documents/STAR%20Institute%20Real-World%20Output%20Templates%20and%20Guidlines%20Tool-kit%20(1).pdf)
- OpenAI's live product page shows one sample deep-research report, "Retail Banking in the United States vs the European Union," written for a prompt that asked for a comparison and a 5-slide executive briefing. The extracted report uses question-shaped headings: "Scope, definitions, and data approach," market overview, customer-acquisition-cost analysis, digital adoption, product penetration, market trends and whitespace, and "Five-slide executive briefing outline," including a recommendations subsection. The help page does not list those headings as a required schema. — [ChatGPT deep research product page](https://chatgpt.com/features/deep-research/); [OpenAI Help, Deep research in ChatGPT](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10500283-deep-research-in-chatgpt)
- OpenAI's help page, which said it was updated 23 days before it was fetched on 11 November 2026, says the finished result is "a structured report with citations or source links." - Google's Gemini Apps help tells the user to edit a research plan, then open the finished report. Export choices on that page are Share Canvas, Export to Docs, and Copy Contents. It does not name methods, findings, limitations, or recommendations as required headings. A separate Docs help page says a Deep Research Google Doc "includes the findings, citations, and an organized summary of the data found." — [Gemini Apps Help, Use Deep Research](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/15719111?hl=en); [Google Docs Editors Help, Use Deep Research in Gemini in Workspace apps](https://support.google.com/docs/answer/17127708?hl=en)
- Perplexity's February 2025 launch post says the agent writes "a clear and comprehensive report" and that the user can export it to a PDF or a document, or turn it into a Perplexity Page. It does not name the report's sections. — [Perplexity, Introducing Perplexity Deep Research, 14 February 2025](https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/blog/introducing-perplexity-deep-research)
- Perplexity's help article on Advanced Deep Research says the report "adapts its depth, tone, and format" to the work, with examples of a thesis, a market analysis, or exploration of a complex topic. The full page fetch timed out on 11 November 2026. This point is from the search extract of that official URL. — [Perplexity Help, What's New in Advanced Deep Research](https://www.perplexity.ai/help-center/en/articles/13600190-what-s-new-in-advanced-deep-research)
- A March 2025 walkthrough of Perplexity Deep Research on a nanomaterials literature review describes sections that match the prompt: emerging nanomaterials, novel fabrication methods, figure of merit, stability and degradation, and a short conclusion. That is a question-shaped outline, recorded in a video summary, not a Perplexity template. — [Lily's summary of Andy Stapleton, 4 March 2025](https://lilys.ai/de/notes/how-to-use-perplexity-20251109/use-perplexity-ai-deep-research-save-time)
- Elicit's help article dated 30 September 2026 offers three report templates: General Review, 1-Page Overview, and Research Gap Analysis, plus a fast, balanced, or comprehensive depth. It does not publish the heading list inside each template. Systematic review reports follow "a report template" chosen on the setup page. — [Elicit Help, Get a research report, 30 September 2026](https://support.elicit.com/en/articles/14756862-get-a-research-report-to-generate-in-depth-answers-automatically-in-elicit); [Elicit Help, Systematic Reviews](https://support.elicit.com/en/articles/14759154-systematic-reviews-in-elicit)

### Inferences
- Optional, in the academic templates, means the assignment or the genre asks for it: recommendations, quotes, a future-research note, and the apparatus of a long report.
- Optional, in these products, means the model invents headings from the question, or the user picks a named template (Elicit) or asks for a structure (the OpenAI banking sample's briefing outline). A heading in one sample is not evidence that the product always emits that heading.

### Gaps
- The internal heading list of Elicit's General Review, 1-Page Overview, and Research Gap Analysis templates was not on the help pages retrieved.
- Perplexity's current help page was not retrieved in full, so the "adapts its format" sentence rests on the search extract.

## Do the product reports include a method note, a disagreement, a confidence note, quotes, or a separate findings list?

### Takeaway
A method note is a required section in a university research report and a short paragraph in a briefing. Among the products, a method is documented in the on-screen plan for OpenAI, Gemini, and Perplexity, and in Elicit's search, screening, and extraction steps. Only Elicit's systematic-review export is documented to put search strategies and a PRISMA flow diagram into the report file. A disagreement section and a confidence note were not found as named parts of any of these product files. Exact quotes are an Elicit feature. A separate findings list is standard in university reports, and is not a documented default heading in the OpenAI, Gemini, or Perplexity files.

### Cited Findings
- University methods sections exist to let another person repeat the work (Wits) or to say how data were collected and analysed (Hull). SFU limits briefing methods to two or three sentences. Discussion is where inconsistencies and comparison with other studies go (Sunshine Coast). Limitations sit in the discussion there, and in the conclusion in the Wits guide. None of these guides names a "confidence" heading. — [Wits](https://www.wits.ac.za/media/wits-university/faculties-and-schools/health-sciences/clinical-medicine/documents/Guidelines%20on%20Writing%20a%20Well%20Structured%20Research%20Report.pdf); [Hull](https://grimsby.ac.uk/documents/highereducation/skills/Report-Writing-A-Brief-Guide-Hull.pdf); [Sunshine Coast](https://libguides.usc.edu.au/scireport/imrad); [SFU](http://www.sfu.ca/content/dam/sfu/starinstitute/Documents/STAR%20Institute%20Real-World%20Output%20Templates%20and%20Guidlines%20Tool-kit%20(1).pdf)
- OpenAI documents a proposed research plan the user can edit before the run, and an activity history in the fullscreen report view. It does not say the plan or the activity history is a "methods" section of the prose. The banking sample includes "Scope, definitions, and data approach," which states definitions and which kinds of sources the comparison uses. That heading matches a prompt that asked for a comparative analysis. — [OpenAI Help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10500283-deep-research-in-chatgpt); [ChatGPT deep research product page](https://chatgpt.com/features/deep-research/)
- Gemini documents an editable research plan before "Start research." Jeanette DePatie Jones, writing about Gemini Deep Research with 2.5 Pro, describes the planning view as showing research focus, search terms, and which resources will be gathered, including a "Research Websites" portion she calls methodology. In finished reports she ran, the prose sometimes stated that sources were gathered with "a multi-pronged approach," and then named source types (wires, trade press, vendor sites). The page discusses a query window ending 23 April 2025 and does not give its own publication date. The image path on the page is dated 2025/04. — [Gemini Apps Help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/15719111?hl=en); [Cottrill Research, Assessing Gemini Deep Research With 2.5 Pro](https://cottrillresearch.com/assessing-gemini-deep-research-with-2-5-pro-focus-on-source-transparency-and-relevancy/)
- Perplexity's launch post describes iterative search and a research plan that changes as the agent reads, then a separate report-writing step. The Advanced Deep Research help extract says key findings appear while research is still running, "so you can start reviewing information before the final report is ready," and that a progress display shows which sources are being read. That places a findings strip in the live run, not in a documented section of the downloaded file. — [Perplexity launch post](https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/blog/introducing-perplexity-deep-research); [Perplexity Help, Advanced Deep Research](https://www.perplexity.ai/help-center/en/articles/13600190-what-s-new-in-advanced-deep-research)
- Elicit Reports, introduced 26 February 2025, are a rapid review: search, screening, and data extraction, then a written report. The 30 September 2026 help article says the same five steps, and says the user can look back at papers found, screening, and the instructions used for extraction. Those steps are the method. The help does not say the PDF or Word report contains a methods chapter. — [Elicit, Introducing Elicit Reports, 26 February 2025](https://elicit.com/blog/introducing-elicit-reports); [Elicit Help, 30 September 2026](https://support.elicit.com/en/articles/14756862-get-a-research-report-to-generate-in-depth-answers-automatically-in-elicit)
- Elicit's systematic-review product, as described on 6 May 2026, exports the PRISMA flow diagram, the search strategies, and a table of characteristics of included studies with the report, and keeps in-line citations in PDF and Word. That is a methods-and-findings apparatus inside the systematic-review file, which is a different Elicit workflow from the lighter Research Report. — [Elicit, Systematic Review built for PRISMA 2020, 6 May 2026](https://elicit.com/blog/evaluating-elicit-slr)
- No OpenAI, Gemini, or Perplexity page retrieved for this note names a disagreement section or a confidence note inside the report file.
- Elicit's 6 May 2026 post says generic tools, naming Claude, ChatGPT, Copilot, and Gemini, downweight contradictory findings and amplify supportive ones. That is Elicit's claim about how those tools behave. It is not a description of a section in their downloaded files. — [Elicit, 6 May 2026](https://elicit.com/blog/evaluating-elicit-slr)
- Elicit states that it "cites every claim with exact quotes from the original source," and the help article says to click inside the report to review supporting quotes and reasoning. The same article says the data-extraction CSV includes supporting quotes and reasoning. Screening can also store "supporting quotes from the paper for every screening decision" in the systematic-review workflow. — [Elicit, 26 February 2025](https://elicit.com/blog/introducing-elicit-reports); [Elicit Help, 30 September 2026](https://support.elicit.com/en/articles/14756862-get-a-research-report-to-generate-in-depth-answers-automatically-in-elicit); [Elicit, 6 May 2026](https://elicit.com/blog/evaluating-elicit-slr)

She does not describe quoted excerpts in that list. — [Cottrill Research](https://cottrillresearch.com/assessing-gemini-deep-research-with-2-5-pro-focus-on-source-transparency-and-relevancy/)

SFU's briefing has a "Research findings" block of up to 600 words, before implications. — [Hull](https://grimsby.ac.uk/documents/highereducation/skills/Report-Writing-A-Brief-Guide-Hull.pdf); [Sunshine Coast](https://libguides.usc.edu.au/scireport/imrad); [SFU](http://www.sfu.ca/content/dam/sfu/starinstitute/Documents/STAR%20Institute%20Real-World%20Output%20Templates%20and%20Guidlines%20Tool-kit%20(1).pdf)

### Inferences
- "Method note" splits in two. The products show the plan and the search trace in the interface. The file the person keeps is not documented to contain that trace, except for Elicit's systematic-review export, which includes search strategies and a flow diagram.

- Elicit's quotes are the only case where a vendor says the report points at the source's words. The help places the click-to-quote action in the report screen, and places the quotes themselves in the CSV export. Whether every quote is also printed in the PDF body is not stated.

### Gaps
- No current official example, fetched in full, shows whether an OpenAI, Gemini, or Perplexity file in November 2026 contains a limitations or confidence paragraph by default.
- The Elicit evaluation page was not opened in full, so the abstract and thematic-analysis remarks are search-extract quotations, not a reading of the whole post.
- CitedSpy and similar pages claim Gemini opens the exact source paragraph in a sidebar. That page was not fetched. It is not used here as a finding.

## What does the source list contain for each source: title, address, date, and a line on what it was used for?

### Takeaway
The university templates require a references section and do not, in the pages read, require a note of what each source was used for. None of the four products is documented as putting title, address, date, and a use-line on every source in the downloaded file. What is documented is thinner, and it differs by product: OpenAI says citations or source links plus a sources-used section; a 2025 Gemini export had numbered linked entries and a date accessed, without publication dates; Perplexity's consumer export is described as a linked list, while its API returns title, URL, snippet, and date; Elicit exports a bibliography file and, separately, quotes and reasoning.

### Cited Findings
- Hull ends the simple report with "References" and the longer report with "References/bibliography." The guide does not itemise author, year, title, URL, or an annotation. SFU counts references as outside the 1,500-word brief. Edinburgh's retrieved text names quotations in the body, not an annotated bibliography. — [Hull](https://grimsby.ac.uk/documents/highereducation/skills/Report-Writing-A-Brief-Guide-Hull.pdf); [SFU](http://www.sfu.ca/content/dam/sfu/starinstitute/Documents/STAR%20Institute%20Real-World%20Output%20Templates%20and%20Guidlines%20Tool-kit%20(1).pdf); [Edinburgh](https://edwebcontent.ed.ac.uk/sites/default/files/atoms/files/how_to_write_a_research_briefing_oct2016.pdf)
- OpenAI's help page says every deep-research output includes "citations or source links," and that the fullscreen view has "a sources used section for reference checking." It does not say each entry has a title, a date, or a sentence on use. The banking sample's extracted text names sources inside the prose (FDIC, Eurostat, Curinos, and others) and does not include a bibliography in the text that was fetched. ### Inferences
- A use-line is the odd field out. Academic reference lists leave use to the citation in the prose. Elicit comes closest, and it puts the quote and the reasoning in a table export and in a click on the report, not in a one-line annotation under each reference.
- Title and address are the fields most often attested for the product lists: Gemini by a 2025 exported works-cited list plus two secondary descriptions, Perplexity by its API and by user descriptions of linked references, Elicit by the existence of a .bib or .ris export. OpenAI's public docs stop at "source links" and "sources used."
- Date is the weakest shared field. Cottrill's Gemini export had access date and not publication date. Perplexity's API has a date field. OpenAI's public docs do not mention one. Elicit's .bib and .ris path can carry a publication year because those are bibliographic formats, but the help article does not say the year is always present.

### Gaps
- No November 2026 download of an OpenAI, Gemini, or Perplexity report was opened for this note, so the source-list fields are from help text, one 2025 Gemini export description, API docs, and user write-ups.
- The university pages read here do not spell out the internal fields of a reference entry, so author, year, title, and locator are not cited from a style manual in this note.
- Whether Elicit's PDF prints a reference list, or only the .bib and .ris files do, is not stated on the export page that was fetched.

## What do these products leave out of the file the person downloads?

### Takeaway
The documented download is the finished prose, with citations, and with tables or images when the report has them. The research plan, the live trace of searches, sources that were read and not cited, and the full text of pages are described as part of the run or the on-screen view, not as part of the export. Elicit is the exception in one direction: its systematic-review file is documented to include the flow diagram, search strategies, and study-characteristic tables, while the lighter report's quotes and screening reasons are a separate CSV.

### Inferences
- For OpenAI, Gemini, and Perplexity, the file is the answer. The record of how the answer was assembled stays in the session. OpenAI comes closest to putting process on screen after the run, because activity history is named in the report view, and even then the PDF description that OpenAI published does not mention it.
- Elicit splits one project into several files. The narrative file is not documented to be the whole audit trail. The systematic-review file is documented to carry more of that trail than the lighter research report.
- Items a university report often has, and that none of these export descriptions claim by default, include a title page with author and date, a methods chapter, a limitations heading, a disagreement or confidence note, and an annotated line on each source. The OpenAI banking sample shows that a prompt can still produce a scope note and recommendations inside the prose.

### Gaps
- OpenAI has not published a field-by-field difference between the fullscreen view and the Markdown, Word, and PDF files. Saying the activity history is omitted from the download is an inference from what the PDF announcement lists, not a documented omission.
- Google has not published whether interactive simulators, the on-screen table of contents, or the research plan appear in the Docs export.
- Perplexity's help page was not fetched in full, and no Perplexity PDF from 2026 was opened, so the contents of today's downloaded file are inferred from the 2025 launch post and from 2025 user descriptions.
- Elicit's help does not say, in the pages fetched, whether quotes are printed in the PDF or only stored for the on-screen click and the CSV.
