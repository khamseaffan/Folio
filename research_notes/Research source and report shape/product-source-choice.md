# How deep research products choose sources

Products covered: OpenAI Deep Research in ChatGPT, Gemini Deep Research, Perplexity Research mode (plus a separate Perplexity Computer page), and two academic tools that are still operating. Elicit help articles are dated 30 September 2026. The Consensus help article was updated 11 November 2026. Both are included because they select sources differently. A direct fetch of Perplexity help pages timed out; Perplexity claims below come from the search extract of those official URLs unless noted.

Vendor means the company published it. Observation means an independent writeup of a real run, or a third-party description of one.

## Does the person name the sources, does the product search and pick, or both?

### Takeaway
The person does not have to name sources. Each product searches and picks. OpenAI and Gemini also let the person restrict or add sources before the run, and both can read files the person uploads. Elicit and Consensus search a paper index rather than the open web, and the person can filter, screen, or upload documents on top of that search.

### Cited Findings
- Vendor, OpenAI: By default, deep research can access the public web and files the person uploads. The person can also connect apps that support deep research, including document stores such as Google Drive or SharePoint, and the run uses those apps' read actions only. In Sites > Manage sites, the person can restrict the run to entered websites or domains, or choose "Prioritize these sites, but allow full-web search." Sites can be entered as a comma-separated list. ChatGPT may ask clarifying questions before research begins, and it proposes a research plan the person can review and edit. — [OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10500283-deep-research-in-chatgpt)
- Vendor, OpenAI: A 10 February 2026 update on the launch post says deep research can connect to any MCP or app and can restrict web searches to trusted sites. The same post, in its original 2 February 2025 body, says the person selects deep research, enters a query, and can attach files or spreadsheets. — [Introducing deep research](https://openai.com/index/introducing-deep-research/)
- Vendor, OpenAI API, not the ChatGPT UI: The Deep Research API model plans sub-questions and uses web search. The cookbook's example prompt tells the model to prioritize peer-reviewed research, health organizations, regulatory agencies, or earnings reports. That is a developer-written instruction in a sample, not a ChatGPT control. — [Deep Research API cookbook](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/deep_research_api/introduction_to_deep_research_api)
- Vendor, Gemini: Google Search is a source by default. The person can add Gmail, Drive, uploaded files, and NotebookLM notebooks. Deselecting Google Search limits the run to the other selected sources. Gmail and Drive require the Google Workspace app to be connected. After submit, Gemini creates a research plan, and the person can click Edit plan before Start research. — [Gemini Apps Help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/15719111?hl=en)
- Vendor, Gemini: The product page says Deep Research autonomously searches and browses the web, and Gmail, Drive, and Chat if the person chooses those. The person can refine the plan. The Gemini API, agent id `deep-research-preview-04-2026`, can return a plan for review when collaborative planning is on, and the caller can also pass documents. — [Gemini Deep Research overview](https://gemini.google/overview/deep-research/); [Gemini API](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/deep-research)
- Vendor, Perplexity: Research mode "performs dozens of searches, reads hundreds of sources, and reasons through the material" and is started from a mode selector. The retrieved extract does not say the person names websites inside Research mode. — [What is Research mode?](https://www.perplexity.ai/help-center/en/articles/10738684-what-is-research-mode)
- Vendor, Perplexity: Advanced Deep Research can process uploaded documents and "browse more of the web." The extract does not describe a site allow-list. — [What's New in Advanced Deep Research](https://www.perplexity.ai/help-center/en/articles/13600190-what-s-new-in-advanced-deep-research)
- Vendor, Perplexity Computer, a different surface from Research mode: The product page says deep research makes a research plan, finds primary sources across hundreds of sites, can use the person's files and apps, and draws on premium sources such as Statista, PitchBook, and CB Insights. This is the Computer product page, retrieved as a search extract of a Spanish-language URL, not a full-page read. — [Perplexity Computer deep research](https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/products/deep-research)
- Vendor, Elicit: The person asks a question. Elicit ranks papers in its own database. The corpus is about 138 million papers from Semantic Scholar, PubMed, and OpenAlex, updated weekly, plus a separate ClinicalTrials.gov corpus. The person can upload documents and connect company subscriptions for more full text. Screening questions and filters, including year, study type, and keyword include or exclude, are optional and person-controlled. — [Elicit paper search](https://elicit.com/solutions/search); [Elicit's source for papers](https://support.elicit.com/en/articles/14758040-elicit-s-source-for-papers); [Improve search results](https://support.elicit.com/en/articles/14758175-improve-search-results)
- Vendor, Consensus: The person can search with a natural-language question, keywords, Boolean operators, a title, an author, or a DOI. Consensus then searches its index of over 400 million papers from Semantic Scholar, OpenAlex, its own scholarly-web crawl, and publisher full-text agreements. A sources dropdown chooses All or Medical Mode, about 8 million papers and 50,000 clinical guidelines. The person can also upload documents and chat with specific papers or collections. — [How Consensus Works](https://help.consensus.app/en/articles/9922673-how-consensus-works); [Consensus Research Database](https://help.consensus.app/en/articles/10055108-consensus-research-database)

### Inferences
- Naming sources is an optional constraint on OpenAI and Gemini, not the default path. If the person does nothing but ask, the product picks from the public web, Google Search in Gemini's case.
- Academic tools invert the web products. The corpus is chosen by the vendor in advance. The person's main lever is the question, filters, and which papers to keep, not which websites to open.
- Perplexity's consumer Research mode, in the help extract, is product-picked web search plus optional uploads. Site restriction shows up in the Computer page and in third-party API catalogs, not in the Research mode help extract.

### Gaps
- Perplexity's own API documentation was not retrieved. A reseller page lists `search_domain_filter` and `max_results`, but that is not treated here as Perplexity's consumer behavior.
- Elicit's "How do you rank sources or determine quality?" FAQ question is on the search page, but the answer text was not in the fetched body.

## What do they search first, and do they follow citations from a source they already opened?

### Takeaway
Web products start with a search, then issue later searches from what they have read. Vendor pages describe clicking, scrolling, and new queries. They do not say the run opens the bibliography of a page it already fetched. Consensus is the exception: its help documents a citation graph and "explore citations and related research" as product features. Elicit uses citation count as a ranking signal, which is not the same as opening a paper's reference list.

### Cited Findings
- Vendor, OpenAI: The launch post says the model was trained to plan and execute a multi-step trajectory, "backtracking and reacting to real-time information where necessary," and to pivot as it encounters information. It cites specific sentences or passages from sources. The system card says training covered searching, clicking, scrolling, and interpreting files. — [Introducing deep research](https://openai.com/index/introducing-deep-research/); [Deep Research system card](https://cdn.openai.com/deep-research-system-card.pdf)
- Vendor, OpenAI: As a prompt-injection control, "we do not allow deep research to navigate to or construct arbitrary URLs." That sentence is about stopping data exfiltration, not about a published citation-following policy. — [Deep Research system card](https://cdn.openai.com/deep-research-system-card.pdf)
- Vendor, Gemini: A 6 March 2025 Workspace post says the plan "starts with general context and then narrows in on details," and "Gemini's searches evolve as it learns more about a topic, just like you might change your keywords after reading a few results." It uses Google's search algorithm "to find quality sources from credible sites." — [Google Workspace blog](https://workspace.google.com/blog/ai-and-machine-learning/meet-deep-research-your-new-ai-research-assistant)
- Vendor, Gemini: The current overview says that at each step the model reasons over information it already has and decides its next move, and that multi-step planning identifies missing information and discrepancies. It does not say the next move is to open references from a page already opened. — [Gemini Deep Research overview](https://gemini.google/overview/deep-research/)
- Vendor, Perplexity: Research mode performs dozens of searches and then reads sources. Advanced Deep Research "searches more sources, cross-references information." Neither extract says it opens outbound citations from a page it already read. The Computer page says it splits a question into subtasks and runs retrieval steps. — [What is Research mode?](https://www.perplexity.ai/help-center/en/articles/10738684-what-is-research-mode); [What's New in Advanced Deep Research](https://www.perplexity.ai/help-center/en/articles/13600190-what-s-new-in-advanced-deep-research); [Perplexity Computer deep research](https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/products/deep-research)
- Vendor, Elicit: Search ranks the whole paper database with custom embedding models by similarity of meaning, also using citation count and recency. It then uses LLMs to re-rank the top 1,000. This is a corpus search, not a web crawl that starts at one paper and follows its references. Without full text, Elicit uses title and abstract. — [Elicit paper search](https://elicit.com/solutions/search); [Elicit's source for papers](https://support.elicit.com/en/articles/14758040-elicit-s-source-for-papers)
- Vendor, Consensus: A normal search casts a hybrid semantic-plus-keyword net over the whole index, then refines with recency, citation count, and journal impact, then re-ranks the top 20 with a larger model. Separately, the product can "explore citations and related research," and a Citation Graph "explore[s] how research papers are connected through citations." For complex tasks, a Research Agent plans searches and chains tools. When full text exists, search can use methods, results, and discussion, not only the abstract. — [How Consensus Works](https://help.consensus.app/en/articles/9922673-how-consensus-works)
- Observation, 24 March 2025: On one identical prompt, Sarah Constantin reported Perplexity using popular medical sites such as WebMD and Medical News Today, Gemini using those plus Healthline, and ChatGPT Deep Research and Elicit using research papers. She does not describe any tool walking a citation chain. — [Sarah Constantin](https://sarahconstantin.substack.com/p/ai-deep-research-tools-reviewed)

### Inferences
- "Follow citations" is documented for Consensus as its own feature. For OpenAI, the documented behavior is browse-and-pivot: search, click, scroll, and change course. That can reach a linked page, but the vendor does not say reference lists are a required next step.
- Gemini's published loop is new searches that get narrower after earlier results, using Google ranking, rather than snowballing from a bibliography.
- Elicit's use of citation count will prefer papers other papers cite. That pulls well-known papers to the top. It does not, on the pages retrieved, open those papers' reference lists.

### Gaps
- No retrieved OpenAI, Gemini, or Perplexity page says whether a run extracts and opens outbound links or reference lists from a page it has already opened.
- The OpenAI system card does not spell out which URLs the click action is allowed to open after the arbitrary-URL block.
- Whether Elicit research reports snowball from citations was not in the retrieved help pages.

## Is there a published or observed cap on how many sources one run opens?

### Takeaway
Vendors publish vague ceilings, not a fixed integer for one ChatGPT, Gemini, or Perplexity run. OpenAI says "hundreds." Gemini says "up to hundreds of websites." Perplexity says dozens of searches and hundreds of sources read. Academic tools publish tighter ranges: Elicit reviews the top 1,000 and shows from 4 to 1,000, and Consensus re-ranks a top 20 for ordinary search and synthesizes 50 to 100 papers in Deep Review. Dated runs usually cite fewer sources than those "hundreds" claims, and several runs report more searches than cited sources.

### Cited Findings
- Vendor, OpenAI: The launch post says ChatGPT will "find, analyze, and synthesize hundreds of online sources." That is not stated as a maximum or a minimum. A chart caption says performance improves as the model browses and thinks more. The post does not give the tool-call ceiling. Help text says the run reads and analyzes "many sources," with no number. — [Introducing deep research](https://openai.com/index/introducing-deep-research/); [OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10500283-deep-research-in-chatgpt)
- Vendor, OpenAI usage, not sources: As of the 24 April 2025 update on the launch post, Plus, Team, Enterprise, and Edu get 25 deep research queries a month, Pro gets 250, and Free gets 5, after which queries switch to a lightweight version. The help page now says the in-product counter shows remaining tasks and a fixed monthly allowance resets 30 days from first use. — [Introducing deep research](https://openai.com/index/introducing-deep-research/); [OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10500283-deep-research-in-chatgpt)
- Vendor, Gemini: The overview says it can "automatically browse up to hundreds of websites" and can process "hundreds of pages of content" in a session, kept in a 1 million token context plus retrieval. Help says a report usually takes 5 to 10 minutes "since Gemini analyzes many sources," and longer for complex reports. No integer cap was found. The API page describes two agents, `deep-research-preview-04-2026` and `deep-research-max-preview-04-2026`, and does not state a source count for either. — [Gemini Deep Research overview](https://gemini.google/overview/deep-research/); [Gemini Apps Help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/15719111?hl=en); [Gemini API](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/deep-research)
- Vendor, Perplexity: Research mode "performs dozens of searches, reads hundreds of sources," and completes most tasks in under 3 minutes, sometimes about 4 to 5 minutes. Advanced Deep Research "searches more sources" and allocates more compute per session. Neither extract gives a hard cap. The Computer page says "thousands" of retrieval steps across "hundreds" of sources. — [What is Research mode?](https://www.perplexity.ai/help-center/en/articles/10738684-what-is-research-mode); [What's New in Advanced Deep Research](https://www.perplexity.ai/help-center/en/articles/13600190-what-s-new-in-advanced-deep-research); [Perplexity Computer deep research](https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/products/deep-research)
- Vendor, Elicit: Embeddings rank the corpus. LLMs then review the top 1,000. Depending on plan and workflow, the product shows "anywhere from the top 4 to the top 1,000." If it judges papers irrelevant, a search can show fewer than 10. — [Elicit paper search](https://elicit.com/solutions/search); [Improve search results](https://support.elicit.com/en/articles/14758175-improve-search-results)
- Vendor, Consensus: Ordinary search ends in a re-rank of the top 20. Deep Review is "an in-depth synthesis of 50–100 papers." These are published ranges for named modes, not a claim about every click. — [How Consensus Works](https://help.consensus.app/en/articles/9922673-how-consensus-works)
- Observation, 24 March 2025, one shared prompt: Perplexity used 15 sources, Gemini 38, ChatGPT 33, Elicit 40. Constantin wrote that the tools are "typically capped at 40 sources." That sentence is her generalization from this comparison, not a vendor limit. — [Sarah Constantin](https://sarahconstantin.substack.com/p/ai-deep-research-tools-reviewed)
- Observation, 8 November 2025, one shared business prompt: ChatGPT reviewed 23 sources and ran 11 searches in 21 minutes. Gemini pulled from 80 sources. Perplexity ran 145 web searches and utilized 32 sources. Smith also writes that Perplexity "claimed '175+ pages'" and produced seven. — [Stephen Smith](https://www.smithstephen.com/p/i-ran-identical-research-through)
- Observation, one Sonar Deep Research API call dated in the log as 10 May 2026: 50 citations, 128.9 seconds. The author says a brief typically has 30 to 50 citations, and that Gemini has the most citations at about 60 to 100 and reports of 20 to 30 pages. Those ranges are the author's summary, not a vendor cap. — [perplexity-deep-research README](https://github.com/dzivkovi/perplexity-deep-research/blob/main/README.md)
- Observation, date not in the retrieved excerpt: Dhruv Tandon, on the prompt "Give me a detailed report on how an IC Engine works," reported OpenAI 27 sources, Perplexity 23, Gemini 122, and Grok 16. — [Deep Research Showdown](https://www.neuralmarkets.decisional.com/p/deep-research-showdown)
- Observation, aggregator, original post and date not verified: A writeup of a comparison reported ChatGPT at 18 sources and 89 searches in 11 minutes, Gemini at about 100 sources, and Perplexity at 98 sources browsed. — [Neura Market excerpt](https://www.neura.market/directories/perplexity/posts/reddit-1qyo0rf)
- Observation, publication date not in the retrieved excerpt: A comparison in The Conversation reported Gemini 206 sources searched and 58 used, Perplexity 51 searched and 5 used, and Elicit 10 searched and 4 used. — [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/estudo-mostra-vantagens-e-limitacoes-do-uso-das-ferramentas-de-pesquisa-profunda-em-trabalhos-academicos-252131)

### Inferences
- "Sources opened" and "sources cited" are different counts. Smith and the unverified aggregator both show ChatGPT and Perplexity running more searches than the number of sources that appear in the answer. Vendor "hundreds" language can describe pages read, while a reader counting footnotes will see a smaller number.
- Constantin's March 2025 "capped at 40" does not survive later observations. Gemini reports of 80, about 100, and 122 sources appear in 2025 and undated later writeups. Treat 40 as one observer's ceiling at that date.
- Elicit and Consensus are the only products here with a published numeric selection stage. Elicit's 1,000 is how many papers get the LLM pass. Consensus's 20 and 50 to 100 are mode-specific, so a Deep Review is not evidence that ordinary search opens 100 papers.

### Gaps
- No retrieved page states a hard maximum of pages opened for one OpenAI, Gemini, or Perplexity consumer run.
- OpenAI's lightweight model, used after the monthly quota in the April 2025 update, has no separate source-count documentation in the pages retrieved.
- The Conversation article date and Tandon's publication date were not in the excerpts retrieved, so those counts cannot be placed in a month.
- Perplexity help pages were not fully fetched, so a cap that exists only in the unretrieved portion of those pages would have been missed.

## How do they avoid a pile of pages that all say the same thing?

### Takeaway
No retrieved vendor page describes a filter whose job is to drop pages because they make the same claim. What is documented instead: Google ranking and narrower follow-up searches at Gemini, cross-referencing at Perplexity, pivot-and-backtrack training at OpenAI, and, for the academic tools, relevance ranking plus an explicit check that a paper is about the question. Consensus also says it will say when evidence is insufficient rather than fill the gap from outside the papers, and it excludes retracted papers.

### Cited Findings
- Vendor, Gemini: Searches start broad and narrow. Later searches change as earlier results come in. Source choice uses Google's search algorithm for "quality sources from credible sites." During synthesis, the model "identifies key themes and inconsistencies" and does multiple passes of self-critique. Planning trades off comprehensiveness against compute and wait time, and looks for missing information and discrepancies. — [Google Workspace blog](https://workspace.google.com/blog/ai-and-machine-learning/meet-deep-research-your-new-ai-research-assistant); [Gemini Deep Research overview](https://gemini.google/overview/deep-research/)
- Vendor, OpenAI: The model is trained to backtrack and react to information it encounters, and to synthesize a large number of websites. The launch post also says it may struggle to distinguish authoritative information from rumors. The API cookbook's sample prompt includes "If you've already retrieved a file, do not call fetch again for that same file." That line is an example instruction a developer can send, not a documented ChatGPT deduplication step. — [Introducing deep research](https://openai.com/index/introducing-deep-research/); [Deep Research system card](https://cdn.openai.com/deep-research-system-card.pdf); [Deep Research API cookbook](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/deep_research_api/introduction_to_deep_research_api)
- Vendor, Perplexity: Advanced Deep Research "cross-references information" and is described as more comprehensive. The extract does not describe near-duplicate removal or a diversity quota. — [What's New in Advanced Deep Research](https://www.perplexity.ai/help-center/en/articles/13600190-what-s-new-in-advanced-deep-research)
- Vendor, Elicit: Overlapping records from Semantic Scholar, PubMed, and OpenAlex are reduced by eliminating incomplete listings, leaving about 138 million papers. That is duplicate-record cleanup, not a check that two papers say the same thing. Ranking is by meaning similarity, citation count, and recency, then an LLM pass on the top 1,000. The person can add screening questions and can exclude keywords. Elicit "will leave out any papers that it thinks are not relevant." — [Elicit's source for papers](https://support.elicit.com/en/articles/14758040-elicit-s-source-for-papers); [Elicit paper search](https://elicit.com/solutions/search); [Improve search results](https://support.elicit.com/en/articles/14758175-improve-search-results)
- Vendor, Consensus: A paper must contain information relevant to the question before it can be included. The product identifies agreement and disagreement, and the Consensus Meter visualizes agreement on yes-or-no questions. If it cannot find sufficient relevant evidence, "it will tell you rather than filling in the gaps with outside information." Retracted papers are excluded from AI analyses. Ranking still uses citation count and journal impact, which favors heavily cited work. — [How Consensus Works](https://help.consensus.app/en/articles/9922673-how-consensus-works)
- Observation, 24 March 2025: Constantin found the main differences were completeness, relevance, and whether sources were papers or commercial health sites, not repeated pages. She does not report a diversity mechanism. Elicit's report on her prompt was "all neurological," which she contrasts with ChatGPT also covering precancerous conditions. — [Sarah Constantin](https://sarahconstantin.substack.com/p/ai-deep-research-tools-reviewed)
- Observation, publication date not in the retrieved excerpt: The Conversation piece says a Gemini report on that test had repetition and a lack of cohesion, and that general web tools did not prioritize academic sources even when the prompt asked for them. — [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/estudo-mostra-vantagens-e-limitacoes-do-uso-das-ferramentas-de-pesquisa-profunda-em-trabalhos-academicos-252131)

### Inferences
- The web products treat redundancy, if they treat it at all, as a later synthesis problem: narrow the next query, cross-check claims, and write themes. They do not publish a step that refuses the third page making the same statement.
- Elicit's similarity ranking can put papers with the same meaning at the top. The documented counters are the LLM re-rank, the relevance drop, and the person's own screening and keyword exclusions.
- Consensus's citation-count and journal signals can cluster on the same well-known literature. The citation graph is the documented way to move to related and later papers, not a diversity quota inside the top 20.

### Gaps
- No retrieved page from OpenAI, Gemini, or Perplexity names a near-duplicate, domain-diversity, or same-claim filter.
- Elicit's quality-ranking FAQ answer was not in the fetched page, so a quality formula beyond similarity, citation count, and recency was not confirmed.

## What does the person see while the run is still choosing?

### Takeaway
OpenAI and Gemini show the selection work before and during the run. OpenAI shows a live progress view and a sidebar of steps and sources, and the person can interrupt to change focus or sources. Gemini shows an editable research plan first, then a thinking panel of what it has learned and what it plans next, and the person can leave and wait for a notification. Perplexity's retrieved help describes the work as dozens of searches and hundreds of sources, but not the on-screen chooser. Elicit and Consensus show ranked papers the person can filter. Their help pages do not describe a live "opening sources" feed for a report run.

### Cited Findings
- Vendor, OpenAI, current help, page retrieved 11 November 2026 and labeled updated 23 days earlier: ChatGPT proposes a research plan the person can review and modify before research begins. While it runs, the person can follow progress and interrupt to refine the focus, including which sources it can access. Afterward, a fullscreen report has a table of contents, a sources-used section, and an activity history of how the research progressed. — [OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10500283-deep-research-in-chatgpt)
- Vendor, OpenAI, launch post: "Once it starts running, a sidebar appears with a summary of the steps taken and sources used." Runs can take 5 to 30 minutes, and the person can leave and get a notification. The 10 February 2026 update says the person can track progress in real time and interrupt with follow-up prompts or new sources. — [Introducing deep research](https://openai.com/index/introducing-deep-research/)
- Vendor, Gemini: The person sees the research plan and can edit it before Start research. Help then says the report usually takes 5 to 10 minutes, the person can leave the chat, and Gemini notifies them in the web app next to the chat thread, or on mobile. The help article's start steps do not describe a live list of sites. — [Gemini Apps Help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/15719111?hl=en)
- Vendor, Gemini: The overview says Deep Research "shows its thoughts as it reasons over information gathered iteratively and thinks before making its next move," and that a thinking panel lets the person follow what the model has learned so far and what it intends to do next. — [Gemini Deep Research overview](https://gemini.google/overview/deep-research/)
- Vendor, Gemini API: With thinking summaries set to auto, the caller can poll or stream a background run. That is the API, not the Gemini app screen. — [Gemini API](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/deep-research)
- Vendor, Perplexity: The Research mode extract says the feature runs searches, reads sources, and reasons, and that most tasks finish in under 3 minutes, sometimes 4 to 5 minutes. It does not describe a plan editor, a source list, or an interrupt control. Advanced Deep Research's extract adds a code sandbox, uploaded documents, and broader browsing, and does not describe the live screen. — [What is Research mode?](https://www.perplexity.ai/help-center/en/articles/10738684-what-is-research-mode); [What's New in Advanced Deep Research](https://www.perplexity.ai/help-center/en/articles/13600190-what-s-new-in-advanced-deep-research)
- Vendor, Perplexity Computer: The product page says the feature creates a research plan. The extract does not describe what is on screen while sources are being chosen. — [Perplexity Computer deep research](https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/products/deep-research)
- Vendor, Elicit: The person sees a results set they can filter and, optionally, screen with their own questions. Search is the visible step. The retrieved pages do not describe a progress feed while a research report is choosing papers. — [Elicit paper search](https://elicit.com/solutions/search); [Improve search results](https://support.elicit.com/en/articles/14758175-improve-search-results)
- Vendor, Consensus: The person searches and then gets a ranked set, with the precision pass applied to the top 20. Deep Review and the Research Agent are separate named features. The help page describes what those features do and does not describe a live activity view while they choose papers. Citations can surface supporting passages when those passages are available. — [How Consensus Works](https://help.consensus.app/en/articles/9922673-how-consensus-works)
- Observation, 8 November 2025: Smith reports wall-clock time and search counts for a finished ChatGPT run, 11 searches and 21 minutes, and a search count for Perplexity, 145. He is reporting what the finished run exposed, not a description of the screen during selection. — [Stephen Smith](https://www.smithstephen.com/p/i-ran-identical-research-through)
- Observation, 24 March 2025: Constantin gives completion times of 2 to 15 minutes depending on the tool, and says none took more than 20 minutes. She does not describe the in-progress UI. — [Sarah Constantin](https://sarahconstantin.substack.com/p/ai-deep-research-tools-reviewed)

### Inferences
- The person can steer OpenAI and Gemini before fetching starts, by editing the plan, and OpenAI also lets them change sources during the run. Gemini's help emphasizes leaving and being notified, while the overview still documents a thinking panel, so both the plan gate and a live thought view are vendor-documented.
- For Perplexity, Elicit, and Consensus, the retrieved official text tells the person the outcome and the method, and it does not document a source-by-source chooser they can watch and interrupt. Absence from the retrieved pages is not proof the screen has no activity list.

### Gaps
- Perplexity help pages were not fully fetched, so a progress UI described only in the unretrieved page body was not confirmed. No 2025 or 2026 independent writeup retrieved here describes Perplexity's screen while a run is still choosing.
- Elicit and Consensus report or agent modes may show progress in the product. That screen was not described in the help pages retrieved.
- OpenAI's launch-post sidebar and the later help-center "activity history" may be the same surface described at two times. The pages do not say whether the sidebar lists every page opened or only a summary.
